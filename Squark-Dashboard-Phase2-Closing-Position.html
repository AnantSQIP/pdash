Defects found and fixed

What was wrongConsequence
"Hours logged" in Performance was always zeroA NULL-unsafe filter excluded every ordinary timesheet entry — all 1,225 of them in the test data. Nobody noticed because zero looks plausible next to a chart.
The ranking rewarded hours and clicks, not outputOne person ranked second in the firm having completed nothing. Eight clicks scored the same as finishing a piece of work.
No record of when a task was finishedEditing a closed task moved it into the current month and could make an on-time delivery retroactively late — and that rate feeds appraisals.
Four concurrency racesThe 16-hour daily cap could be breached (24h recorded), and duplicate leave, WFH and department records could all be created.
Editing a policy kept every old acknowledgement"27 people accepted this" could mean 27 people accepted a different document.
Signing out did not end the sessionThe access token stayed usable for up to fifteen minutes.
Billable % showed 0% for HR and BDTheir hours sat in the denominator but could never be in the numerator. Not a worse performance — a different kind of work.
A deploy could take the whole site downA missing flag silently blanked every secret; the error on screen talked about health, not configuration. It cost an outage.

After this deploy the two flags stop being necessary. The compose file now takes its secrets from .env.production directly instead of interpolating them, so a future deploy is just docker compose -f docker-compose.prod.yml up -d --build — and it fails loudly if the environment file is missing, rather than blanking every secret and taking the site down. Keep using both flags for this run; they are harmless, and the change only takes effect once the new file is on the server.
